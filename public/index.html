        Send-notice deadlines

        The last day to send notice so a contract doesn’t renew on its own: end of the current term minus the notice period in the clause. Order: missed, due within 30 days, needs review, then time bands (31–90 days, 91–365, over a year). Inside a band, the longer renewal goes first even if its date is later.

            Method and accuracyHow dates are worked out and how often they are right

            Extraction is deterministic: regular expressions plus date and duration parsing (“sixty (60) days”, “one hundred and eighty (180) days”, “Eighty-Four (84) months”). No language model and no network call touches your documents; uploads stay in this browser tab. A term of N months “commencing on” a date ends the day before the anniversary; “until N years following” ends on the anniversary. When words and numerals disagree, or a value is redacted, the field is left blank and marked for review.

            Accuracy, stated plainly: it is tested on real SEC contracts it has not seen, and the answers are worked out by hand first. It is often right and it is sometimes wrong. Latest blind test, 17 SEC contracts it had never seen: 5 dates right, 4 correctly left blank, 7 missed and 1 wrong. None of the wrong dates was marked Sure. The wrong card took its start from a date in the background section and was marked Check. A date is marked Sure only when the start date is written in the term clause, the renewal rule is simple, the notice period is stated, and a second count gives the same date. When it can’t tell, it leaves the date blank and says why, and anything resting on an assumption is marked Check. Read the quoted clause before you rely on a date (details).

            Real contracts are material agreements filed with the SEC, fetched from sec.gov with a declared User-Agent under SEC fair-access rules. Send-notice deadlines that land on a weekend or a US federal holiday get a “send by” business day: weekends and US federal holidays flagged; local holidays not modelled. The contract’s own date is always shown unchanged. Dates projected past the initial term assume the agreement was not terminated or amended. Not legal advice.
