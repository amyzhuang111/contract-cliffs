    Send-notice deadlines

    The last day to send notice so a contract doesn’t renew on its own: end of the current term minus the notice period in the clause. Order: missed, due within 30 days, needs review, then time bands (31–90 days, 91–365, over a year). Inside a band, the longer renewal goes first even if its date is later.

      Method and accuracy · How dates are worked out and how often they are right

      Extraction is deterministic: regular expressions plus date and duration parsing (“sixty (60) days”, “one hundred and eighty (180) days”, “Eighty-Four (84) months”). No language model and no network call touches your documents; uploads stay in this browser tab. A term of N months “commencing on” a date ends the day before the anniversary; “until N years following” ends on the anniversary. When words and numerals disagree, or a value is redacted, the field is left blank and marked for review.

      Accuracy, stated plainly: tuned on the 10 example SEC contracts (all hand-checked). On a test set of 10 more EDGAR agreements it had never seen, run once before any change: 3 send-notice deadline dates right, 6 correctly left blank for review, 1 missed, 0 wrong send-notice deadline dates, plus one wrong "current term" date that has since been fixed. An earlier set of 14 is now tuning data (first pass there: 2 right, 5 flagged, 6 missed, 1 wrong) (details).

      Real contracts are material agreements filed with the SEC, fetched from EDGAR with a declared User-Agent under SEC fair-access rules. Send-notice deadlines that land on a weekend or a US federal holiday get a “send by” business day: weekends and US federal holidays flagged; local holidays not modelled. The contract’s own date is always shown unchanged. Dates projected past the initial term assume the agreement was not terminated or amended. Not legal advice.
